Lost and relocated are different things and the distinction is the
whole result. A drain closes a connection that is between transactions
with `57P01`, which every mainstream driver answers by reconnecting;
that costs a reconnect and no work, and it is the relocated column.
Lost is a transaction that had already started. Counting the two
together, which the load client used to do, makes "zero failed
transactions" a target a working drain can never hit.

Run 2 is the control. It is the same load against the same fleet with
the drain skipped, so its losses are what run 1's zero is a claim
against. A rehearsal with only the first row cannot tell a working
drain from an idle load generator.

## What ran

- 150s of `bin/pgload` at 40 connections against the Service,
  replaying `product/perf/workload.yaml`
- three proxy nodes, `drain.graceSeconds` of 10, one Postgres
- the restart begins 25s in, after the connections have settled, so
  what is measured is a restart rather than a startup

## What this does not cover

One node's clients move to two others on a single machine. It does not
say what happens when the fleet is at its connection cap and a third of
it goes away at once, which is the case where shedding has to work and
where a rehearsal on real hardware would be worth running.
